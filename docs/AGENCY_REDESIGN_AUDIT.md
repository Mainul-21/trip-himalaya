# Trip Himalaya public redesign audit — 15 August 2026

The current site already has a coherent navy-and-saffron palette, practical booking paths, local Dharamshala positioning, and a readable public hierarchy. The redesign must preserve those working foundations rather than replace the site with a generic template.

The first visual review identified three priorities. First, public pages overuse condensed uppercase display typography, rounded cards, badges, and orange calls to action; the next design pass should reserve these treatments for genuinely important moments. Second, the homepage can become faster to scan by making its hero, discovery, and enquiry sections more concise. Third, public tours must use only credible travel imagery: the current illustrated tricolour placeholder-style images are not suitable for an authentic professional agency and must be replaced with genuine Himachal/Dharamshala photography or withheld until an administrator supplies a real photo.

The refreshed visual system will use a narrow set of elements: deep Himalayan navy, warm saffron as a restrained action colour, off-white surfaces, one compact display face for selected headings, and a plain sans-serif for all information. It will retain clear booking and enquiry calls to action, but remove generic visual noise and avoid fabricated reviews, trust claims, ratings, statistics, certifications, or generated-looking imagery.

## Post-refinement visual review — 15 August 2026

The refreshed homepage, catalogue, About page, and contact page were reviewed at desktop and phone breakpoints. The fixed header, practical orange planning action, deep-navy contact surfaces, concise copy blocks, and clear enquiry form retain a coherent agency presentation at smaller widths. The redesigned page hierarchy is more spacious and direct without removing working discovery, booking, enquiry, search, or administrative routes.

The incomplete administrator test journey was retained as an unpublished, unfeatured draft so it no longer appears on the public catalogue or homepage. The remaining Triund illustration was replaced with the project’s existing managed hiking and camp photographs while preserving all tour text, pricing, and settings.

## Final public QA — 15 August 2026

The final desktop and phone checks confirmed a restrained, responsive agency presentation across the homepage, catalogue, About page, and Triund detail page. The sticky header remains compact on a phone; the practical planning action stays visible without blocking content. The tour detail page uses a clear hero, readable information rhythm, practical booking panel, and genuine managed mountain photography.

No reviews, ratings, customer names, statistics, certifications, awards, or other trust claims were fabricated during the redesign.
